# Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) — Overview

## What is TPMS?

TPMS stands for Tire Pressure Monitoring System. It is an electronic system that monitors the air pressure in a vehicle's tires and warns the driver when pressure drops significantly below the vehicle manufacturer's recommended level.

Properly inflated tires improve fuel efficiency, extend tire life, and reduce the risk of blowouts. Under-inflation is the leading cause of tire failure and contributes to approximately 11,000 tire-related crashes annually in the United States.

## Why It Matters

| Benefit | Impact |
|---|---|
| Fuel economy | Under-inflated tires can reduce fuel efficiency by up to 3% |
| Tire life | Correct pressure extends tread life by 4,700+ miles on average |
| Safety | Reduces risk of tire failure and loss of vehicle control |
| CO₂ reduction | Proper inflation reduces CO₂ emissions by ~1 ton/vehicle/year |

## How the Dashboard Light Works

- **Solid amber TPMS light**: One or more tires are significantly under-inflated. Check and inflate all tires to the recommended pressure.
- **Flashing TPMS light (60–90 seconds at startup, then solid)**: System malfunction. Visit a service center for diagnosis.
- **Light turns off after inflating**: System has reset successfully. No further action required.

## For Fleet Operators

Fleet managers can monitor TPMS data across an entire vehicle fleet through a central telematics platform. Integration capabilities include:

- **Real-time alerts**: Notifications sent immediately when a vehicle's tire pressure drops below threshold
- **Maintenance scheduling**: Automated work order creation for vehicles with persistent pressure issues
- **Reporting**: Monthly tire health reports by vehicle, route, or depot
- **API access**: TPMS telemetry available via REST API for integration with existing fleet management software

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive with the TPMS light on?**
A: If the light is solid, you should stop and check your tire pressure as soon as safely possible. Driving on significantly under-inflated tires risks tire damage or failure.

**Q: Why did my TPMS light come on in cold weather?**
A: Tire pressure decreases approximately 1 PSI for every 10°F drop in temperature. The light may come on during cold snaps even without a leak — inflate tires to the recommended level.

**Q: How often should TPMS sensors be replaced?**
A: Sensor batteries typically last 5–10 years. Plan for replacement at the 7-year mark or when the system shows a sensor fault code.

**Q: Do I need to reset TPMS after rotating tires?**
A: With direct TPMS, yes — a relearn procedure is required after rotation so the system maps each sensor to the correct wheel position.
